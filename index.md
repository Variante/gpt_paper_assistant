### 1001\. [DiWALI: Diffusion Policy Learning in World Models via Latent Action Inference from Human and Robot Play](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24836)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24836 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24836)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24836.pdf)]

**Authors:** Akshay L Chandra, Iman Nematollahi, Lingyue Zhao, ..., Fabien Despinoy, Abhinav Valada

**Abstract:** Learning robot manipulation is constrained by the availability of action-labeled robot data, for both imitation and world models. Humans can demonstrate skills in seconds, but their demonstrations lack the required robot actions. We introduce DiWALI, a framework that learns and improves diffusion policies within a world model trained on human and robot play. DiWALI infers latent actions from consecutive frames using an inverse dynamics model, enabling world model training without action labels, and grounds them in robot commands through a decoder trained separately on robot play. Skill policies are trained over latent actions from action-free human demonstrations and improved with reinforcement learning in imagination. On the CALVIN benchmark, DiWALI policies trained solely from human demonstrations substantially outperform baselines trained with robot demonstrations. Incorporating human play into the world model training also yields stronger policies after fine-tuning than using the same amount of robot play alone. Experiments on a physical robot show that skills learned primarily from human demonstrations are further improved in imagination with a world model trained on human and robot play. Together, these results demonstrate that human experience can support both manipulation skill acquisition and subsequent policy improvement, reducing reliance on action-labeled robot data. Videos, dataset and code are available at https://diwali.cs.uni-freiburg.de.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: DiWALI infers latent actions from unlabeled human and robot play and improves diffusion policies for manipulation through reinforcement learning in a world model, with results on CALVIN and a physical robot.

### 1002\. [SHAPE: Discrete Supervision and Parallel Continuous Streams for Learning Dexterous Manipulation from Egocentric Human Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24585)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24585 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24585)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24585.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yaping Li, Zhihong Liu, Jiacheng Li, ..., Dahua Lin, Zhijie Deng

**Abstract:** Egocentric human recordings are far cheaper than dexterous robot demonstrations, promising stronger pretraining and direct skill learning without task-specific robot data. Yet humans and robots differ in morphology and kinematics, so human recordings lack the joint positions and actuator commands that supervise robot policies, providing only retargeted end-effector (EEF) actions. This raises two questions: how human data should supervise the policy, and how a dexterous robot should read out actions at deployment. We address both in SHAPE (Supervision from Human Action tokens with Parallel Executable streams), a vision-language-action (VLA) model whose backbone predicts discrete action tokens of EEF chunks as an auxiliary task, while a shared flow-matching expert outputs two executable streams, EEF and joint. On a bimanual robot with 22-DoF hands, a matched study shows that discrete supervision helps learn from human data, driving backbone gradients and shaping action-informative representations across domains. Under standard control pipelines at deployment, direct joint readout excels at fine-grained control with robot demonstrations, scoring 0.80 across six tasks against 0.63 for π0.5, whereas IK-based EEF readout excels at human-to-robot transfer. Pretrained with human data and discrete tokens, SHAPE learns new tasks from human demonstrations without task-specific robot data.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: SHAPE uses a vision-language-action model with discrete action supervision and two flow-matched control streams to learn dexterous bimanual tasks from human demonstrations without task-specific robot data.

### 1003\. [HINT-WAM: Hindsight-Informed Training for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24789)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24789 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24789)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24789.pdf)]

**Authors:** Phuong Tuan Dat, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang

**Abstract:** World action models (WAMs) have shown promising generalization and data efficiency in robot manipulation by coupling action generation with predictions of future visual dynamics. Translating these capabilities into reliable task execution, however, remains a challenge for existing post-training methods. Offline supervised fine-tuning provides dense supervision on expert trajectories, but its flow-matching action expert is trained only on noised demonstration actions and never on the intermediate states produced by its own sampler; task-specific adaptation can also compromise pretrained capabilities. On-policy reinforcement learning trains on the policy's own behavior, yet sparse task rewards make exploration and credit assignment difficult, while dense reward shaping still provides scalar evaluations rather than direct action targets. We introduce \textbf{HINT-WAM}, the first on-policy self-distillation framework that supervises the action expert on its own sampling trajectories using hindsight, the future video of the demonstration, as privileged information available to the WAM itself. For each demonstration observation, the student generates its action through its own denoising trajectory, and at the states along this trajectory the same model acts as a teacher conditioned additionally on this hindsight, providing a conditional action distribution as a dense training target, while the student sees only deployment-time inputs and needs no environmental rewards or simulator interaction. To transfer this supervision to a flow-matching action expert, HINT-WAM distills the teacher into the student with an energy score, a distribution-level objective that matches the teacher's action distribution from the student's own samples rather than regressing its velocity field. The project web is available in https://tuandattt.github.io/hintwam/.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: HINT-WAM uses future demonstration video to supervise a robot manipulation world action model along its own action-denoising trajectories, without environmental rewards or simulator interaction.

### 1005\. [Grana: Compositional Reward Modeling for Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24926)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24926 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24926)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24926.pdf)]

**Authors:** Zi-ang Cao, Kaiyuan Zheng, Yinzhen Xu, ..., Guanzhi Wang, Yuke Zhu

**Abstract:** Robot reward modeling faces two challenges: generalizing across tasks and accurately tracking progress in long-horizon manipulation. General-purpose reward models demonstrate broad task transfer, but transfer from short-task pretraining to long-horizon tasks remains underexplored. Long-horizon progress models often rely on task-specific subtask classifiers or fixed action vocabularies, limiting the reuse of learned progress knowledge when adapting to new tasks. To address these challenges, we introduce Grana, a vision-language robot reward model that learns to identify unfinished subtasks and estimate their continuous progress using open-vocabulary subtask descriptions. We train a language-conditioned model to estimate progress in short-horizon tasks and individual subtasks of long-horizon tasks. For long-horizon tasks, we combine the selected subtask's predicted progress with credit for preceding completed subtasks to estimate whole-task progress. General progress prediction requires training the model across diverse tasks with progress supervision that accounts for imperfect execution. Elapsed-time proxies can misrepresent progress when relative subtask durations vary, or failures reverse local progress. We therefore introduce an annotation protocol that converts sparse subtask boundaries and action-quality annotations into dense progress targets, without requiring annotators to assign numerical progress scores. Pretraining with this supervision on 200 diverse, mostly short-horizon manipulation tasks enables zero-shot reward prediction on unseen tasks and improves progress prediction after adaptation to multi-minute, long-horizon tasks. Experiments demonstrate (i) generalizable reward prediction, including adaptation to five long-horizon tasks, (ii) benefits from pretraining and composing subtask progress estimates, and (iii) improved downstream policy performance.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Grana uses a vision-language reward model to identify unfinished subtasks from open-vocabulary descriptions and compose their progress estimates, improving downstream manipulation policy performance.

### 1006\. [Phoenix: Learning Scene Structure Representations for Generalizable World-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24938)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24938 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24938)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24938.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yupeng Zheng, Chaoyue Li, Linbo Wang, ..., Shuicheng Yan, Dongbin Zhao

**Abstract:** World-action models jointly predict future observations and actions, yet reconstruction-oriented visual representations do not explicitly emphasize the spatial relationships and object-state changes needed for manipulation. We introduce Phoenix, which learns representations of scene structure for world-action modeling. Specifically, Phoenix Encoder enriches DINOv3's general visual semantics through dual-teacher distillation of geometric and spatiotemporal semantic knowledge. Next, CS-VAE causally aggregates and compresses Phoenix Encoder features into generative latents while preserving scene information and spatial layout. Finally, Phoenix jointly predicts these latents, complementary appearance latents, and robot actions, linking scene evolution with action generation. To study pretraining data efficiency, we curate approximately 39,000 hours of egocentric and robot manipulation data for representation learning and use the robot demonstration subset for WAM pretraining. Following benchmark-specific post-training, Phoenix achieves state-of-the-art performance across simulation and real-robot benchmarks spanning single-arm, bimanual, and dexterous manipulation. It demonstrates strong robustness to perturbations, achieving success rates of 94.8% on LIBERO-Plus and 72.4% on RoboTwin 2.0 C2R, alongside strong cross-embodiment transfer and real-world manipulation performance on Bench2Dex and RoboDojo-RealWorld, respectively. Pretraining-scale analysis shows larger performance gains from data scaling than a video-VAE-only baseline, indicating stronger scaling benefits and more effective data utilization. Moreover, Phoenix maintains high success rates when adapted with limited downstream demonstrations, demonstrating strong few-shot adaptation. Our code, pretraining data, and models are available on the project page to help the community reproduce our results.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Phoenix jointly predicts scene-structure latents, appearance latents, and robot actions in a world-action model that achieves 94.8% success on LIBERO-Plus.

### 1007\. [$Z$-sight: Unifying Vision-Language-Action and Latent World Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24940)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24940 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24940)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24940.pdf)]

**Authors:** Junjie Ye, Yiqi Zhao, Muchen Xu, ..., Yue Wang, Yuliang Guo

**Abstract:** We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling. Video-based world action models (WAMs) show the value of joint future-and-action generation for control, yet inherit the heavy cost of video generators. Recent latent-space policies avoid pixel generation, yet typically align intermediate features or rely on frozen target encoders, decoupling future prediction from the VLM's native feature space. $Z$-sight instead predicts the future directly in the visual token space in which its VLM perceives the present. Coupling a foresight expert and a continuous action expert with the VLM in a mixture-of-transformers, $Z$-sight jointly denoises future spatial tokens and actions at inference without pixel reconstruction or external encoders. Trained on human video and robot trajectories, $Z$-sight achieves 81.9% success on SIMPLE (+9.6 points over the strongest baseline) and 73.3% across six Unitree G1 tasks (+20.0 points) at 110 ms per chunk, while remaining competitive on tabletop benchmarks.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Z-sight jointly denoises future visual tokens in its VLM's native feature space and continuous robot actions, achieving 81.9% success on SIMPLE.

### 1010\. [Render the Robot, Generate the World: One Video World Model Across Embodiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24925)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24925 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24925)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24925.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yash Jangir, Karan Mirakhor, Renos Zabounidis, ..., Yonatan Bisk, Homanga Bharadhwaj

**Abstract:** Predicting the consequences of an agent's actions requires modeling both the body's motion and the environment's response. Most video world models attempt to learn both from numerical actions, even though geometry and kinematics already specify the intended motion. We introduce Doppel, an embodiment-agnostic video world model conditioned on camera-aligned renderings of future body configurations and numerical action trajectories. By explicitly providing the intended motion, Doppel can focus on predicting object motion and interaction-driven changes in the environment. Rendered motion also provides a common visual interface across different embodiments and action spaces. To train on robot datasets without camera extrinsics, we recover camera poses and align rendered trajectories with recorded observations. We jointly train a single shared video backbone and conditioning adapter on eight datasets spanning eight robot and human embodiments, from single- and bimanual robot arms to a dexterous humanoid and human hands and bodies, using about 600 hours of video without embodiment labels. Across three robot-arm datasets, Doppel improves PSNR over action-only baselines by 1.00 dB on held-out trajectories and 1.20 dB under out-of-distribution evaluation, while predicting more consistent object motion. Training on all eight embodiments (Doppel-8) improves robot predictions over the three-robot model (Doppel-3) on four of six evaluation sets. Project page: https://doppelwm.github.io/.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Doppel is a generative video world model for robot manipulation that conditions on rendered robot motion and improves out-of-distribution PSNR by 1.20 dB over action-only baselines across three robot-arm datasets.

### 1011\. [HandWorldBench: A Unified Benchmark for Hand-Centric World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24731)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24731 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24731)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24731.pdf)]

**Authors:** Zhaolu Wang, Jiacheng Xu, Lihong Wang, ..., Yong Li, Zhibo Chen

**Abstract:** Recent advances in world models are opening new possibilities for fine-grained embodied tasks, with hand-object interaction offering a particularly demanding setting. Modeling these interactions involves capturing how hand actions change objects and how the resulting states persist over time. Yet systematic evaluation tailored to these hand-centric capabilities remains underdeveloped. To address this gap, we introduce HandWorldBench, a unified benchmark that systematically evaluates hand-centric world models across human hands, simulated dexterous hands, and real dexterous hands along six dimensions: visual quality, hand fidelity, appearance consistency, instruction following, physical consistency, and temporal coherence. To construct the benchmark, we develop a data curation pipeline that extracts hand-object interaction clips and screens them for quality, yielding 20,500 high-quality egocentric clips from over 600 hours of footage. We then manually select 500 clips covering seven task families for evaluation. Benchmarking eleven models under a unified protocol reveals that stronger hand-pose control does not consistently translate into coherent object-state evolution and persistence or robust cross-embodiment performance, with several specialized hand-control models underperforming general-purpose text/image-to-video generators on dexterous-hand tasks.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: HandWorldBench evaluates 11 generative world models on hand–object manipulation across human, simulated robotic, and real robotic hands, finding that stronger hand-pose control does not reliably yield coherent object-state changes.

### 1013\. [RoboMorpher: Scaling Robot Morphology Representations for Cross-Embodiment Action Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24816)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24816 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24816)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24816.pdf)]

**Authors:** Wenwei Lin, Yan Qin, Weiliang Deng, ..., Ping Luo, Tianxing Chen

**Abstract:** Learning shared action representations is central to cross-embodiment manipulation. However, identical joint values can produce different spatial configurations across robots, limiting representations learned from joint values alone. We introduce RoboMorpher, a morphology-conditioned action tokenizer that jointly reconstructs joint angles and link poses to associate control coordinates with their kinematic effects. MorphGen supplies scalable kinematic supervision by generating serial-arm descriptions from survey-derived geometric priors. We pretrain RoboMorpher on 40 million morphology-state pairs and integrate the frozen tokenizer into a vision-language-action policy for discrete prediction and continuous refinement. We also introduce EmbodiBench, a ten-task benchmark with 30 generated URDFs per task. With downstream URDFs excluded from tokenizer pretraining, the policy achieves 56.40% success on RoboTwin 2.0 and 23.27% on EmbodiBench, exceeding continuous joint-space control by 9.73 and 15.34 percentage points. Removing link-pose supervision reduces EmbodiBench success by 17.94 percentage points, supporting morphology-aware geometric supervision for joint-action representation learning.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: RoboMorpher integrates a morphology-conditioned action tokenizer into a vision-language-action manipulation policy, improving success over continuous joint-space control by 15.34 percentage points on EmbodiBench.

### 2004\. [ReCoCo: From Patches to Compositions for Visual Representation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24806)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24806 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24806)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24806.pdf)]

**Authors:** Juan Gutiérrez, Saman Zadtootaghaj, José Luis Blanco-Murillo, Sara Atito

**Abstract:** Self-supervised learning has produced powerful visual representations, yet learning strong dense features remains challenging. While consistency between augmented views provides strong image-level supervision, extending this principle to local visual content is less straightforward. Existing approaches often match individual patches across views or identify coherent regions, introducing uncertainties in correspondence estimation and assumptions about how visual content should be grouped. We introduce ReCoCo (Regional Composition Consistency), a principled approach that extends cross-view consistency to the regional level without requiring such correspondences or semantic grouping. ReCoCo represents the content shared between augmented views through compositions of their patch-level predictions over learned visual prototypes. By aligning these compositions, the model learns from regions containing multiple visual concepts without requiring them to be separated beforehand. ReCoCo integrates naturally with teacher-student self-distillation and introduces no additional learned parameters. Extensive experiments demonstrate substantial improvements in frozen-feature segmentation, clustering, and regional retrieval while maintaining competitive image-level recognition, alleviating the trade-off between dense and global representation quality.

**Comment:** Criterion 2: ReCoCo aligns regional compositions of patch-level prototype predictions across augmented views, improving frozen-feature segmentation and retrieval without adding learned parameters.

### 2009\. [Reconstruction vs. Prediction Pre-Training of Discrete Video Representations for Continuous Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24781)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24781 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24781)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24781.pdf)]

**Authors:** Ron Kosova, Francesco Croce

**Abstract:** Self-supervised video encoders are commonly trained to either reconstruct pixels or predict latent features, yet it remains unclear how these objectives shape representations for downstream dynamics. We present a capacity-matched study isolating this choice: two identical video encoders with a shared FSQ bottleneck are trained under autoencoding and V-JEPA objectives on a continuous control environment. We introduce a simple asymmetric discretization scheme to apply the same bottleneck to the predictive model. The resulting representations exhibit a clear trade-off: the autoencoder preserves substantially more physical state information and achieves slightly better one-step latent prediction, while the predictive encoder accumulates less error under autoregressive rollout. Structural probes suggest that this advantage is associated with greater robustness to low-level visual perturbations and stronger dependence on temporal context. These results indicate that predictive and reconstructive objectives induce distinct representations of dynamics, trading state fidelity for properties that may benefit long-horizon prediction.

**Comment:** Criterion 2: A capacity-matched comparison of pixel reconstruction and V-JEPA finds that reconstruction preserves more physical state information, while predictive pretraining yields less error over autoregressive rollouts.

### 4008\. [ALIVE: Bring Foundation Features to Life with Events](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24579)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24579 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24579)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24579.pdf)]

**Authors:** Rong Zou, Haiqing Hao, Davide Scaramuzza

**Abstract:** Visual foundation models provide powerful representations for diverse tasks, but their features become stale as visual content changes between image observations. Refreshing them requires new frames and repeated runs of a computationally expensive image encoder. We introduce ALIVE, an event-based method that incrementally updates native foundation model features between image observations. Given an anchor image, ALIVE uses subsequent events to transport and correct existing multi-layer features extracted by a frozen DINOv3 encoder. Its lightweight updater is trained end-to-end using only synthetic data, without downstream task supervision, and transfers directly to real-world event streams without any adaptation. Across three real-world datasets and five task-dataset settings spanning object detection, semantic segmentation, and depth estimation, ALIVE outperforms all evaluated event-based methods in every metric. On DSEC, when the anchor frame is 200,ms old, ALIVE improves over anchor-image predictions by 30.9,mAP in detection, 11.6,mIoU in segmentation, and 15.9% in depth AbsRel. Compared with image re-encoding, ALIVE reduces FLOPs by 82.4% and latency by a factor of 2.5.

### 5000\. [DrawScore: Evaluating Generated CAD Models against Engineering Drawings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24681)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24681 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24681)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24681.pdf)]

**Authors:** Haorui Chen, Shiteng Lu, Zhanyu Guo, ..., Xiande Huang, Rui Huang

**Abstract:** Automatic CAD generation is commonly evaluated with volumetric IoU, Chamfer Distance, and surface F-score, which barely penalize local errors that occupy only a small fraction of a part's volume or surface area, such as a misplaced hole or a slot on the wrong face, even though such errors are critical to manufacturing. We formalize these metrics in a unified integral form and show that the weight of a local error decays as the $(k+q)$-th power of its size, where $k$ is the dimension of the integration domain and $q$ is determined by the penalty function. Guided by this analysis, we propose DrawScore, which projects both models into three orthographic views with visible and hidden lines, as in engineering drawings, and applies a saturating penalty along the projected curves, so that the weight of a local error decays only linearly. Controlled experiments confirm the predicted decay orders, and on outputs of existing CAD generation agents, DrawScore exposes errors in outputs that IoU rates as nearly perfect. DrawScore further improves generation as a reinforcement learning reward and, for CAD generation from engineering drawings, serves as a test-time verifier without 3D ground truth or an additional reward model. Our code is available at https://github.com/UESTC-Simon/DrawScore.

**Comment:** Criterion 5: DrawScore evaluates the fidelity of generated 3D CAD models using three orthographic projections and exposes misplaced holes and slots that volumetric IoU can miss.

### 5012\. [UltraTex: Aligning Physical Knowledge for High-Fidelity PBR Texture Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24941)
**ArXiv:** 2611.24941 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24941)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24941.pdf)]

**Authors:** Jingzhi Bao, Xiaohe Ma, Hong Li, Yuanming Hu, Xiaoguang Han

**Abstract:** Generating physically based rendering (PBR) texture maps for a 3D mesh from a single reference image is highly ambiguous, as the observed appearance entangles material identity, surface reflectance, and illumination. While current generative methods produce realistic albedo maps, they still struggle to infer accurate metallic and roughness attributes for relighting. We present UltraTex, a cascaded multi-view framework that moves beyond appearance matching by integrating physical knowledge into multi-view diffusion-based PBR generation. UltraTex first jointly predicts albedo and world-space shading normals to generate more detailed albedo maps, and then predicts metallic-roughness (MR) maps conditioned on the generated albedo, reference image, and material semantic priors. To inject material semantics, a lightweight adapter transforms hidden-layer features of a vision-language model (VLM) into intrinsic material representation tokens, providing part-level knowledge anchors for MR estimation. To model material-light interaction, photometric representation alignment guides intermediate diffusion features toward ground-truth material renderings under controlled lighting. This makes metallic and roughness variations observable through highlight intensity, specular sharpness, and local reflectance contrast, providing stronger guidance for fine-grained material prediction without explicit lighting supervision. Extensive experiments show that UltraTex generates high-quality textures and outperforms existing open-source and commercial methods in visual fidelity, material accuracy, and physical plausibility.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 5: UltraTex uses multi-view diffusion and controlled-lighting feature alignment to generate relightable PBR textures for existing 3D meshes, outperforming open-source and commercial methods in material accuracy and physical plausibility.
