### 1000\. [What Should Robot Policies Learn to Predict? Rethinking Supervision Targets in Vision-Language-Action Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21915)
**ArXiv:** 2610.21915 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21915)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21915.pdf)]

**Authors:** Haitao Shen, Hanyu Liu, Yihan Lin, ..., Wenxin Li, Yuan Yao

**Abstract:** Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are typically trained with action prediction as the primary supervision signal, since robots ultimately execute actions. Auxiliary objectives such as latent-action (LA) and future-vision (FV) prediction are often added to improve policy learning, yet they compete with action prediction for the same training budget. We ask whether, under a fixed budget, allocating a substantial share of training samples to such objectives, rather than to further action prediction, can improve policy learning. We build a unified VLA framework that couples a two-stream multimodal diffusion transformer (MMDiT) with a sample-level router, which assigns each training sample to either action prediction or an auxiliary objective and thus makes supervision allocation explicit. Within this framework, we instantiate three LA targets based on LAPA and its DINO-feature variants, and three FV targets based on DINOv3, V-JEPA 2, and Wan2.2 representations. Across LIBERO, CALVIN, and four real-robot tasks, suitable LA and FV supervision outperforms action-only training under the same budget, even when action prediction receives no more than half of the training samples. FV generally outperforms LA, although the strongest LA target remains competitive on CALVIN. Diagnostic analyses suggest that DINO-based LA targets emphasize relative motion, whereas FV targets emphasize absolute position and task identity. We therefore argue that what robot policies learn to predict, and how training is allocated among these targets, should be treated as explicit design choices rather than defaulting to action-dominant training.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 1: a diffusion-transformer VLA framework routes training samples between action prediction and auxiliary latent-action or future-vision prediction, improving manipulation performance over action-only training under equal budgets on LIBERO, CALVIN, and four real-robot tasks.

### 1001\. [TS-WAM: Temporal Latent Actions and World-Action Coupling for Vision-Language Robot Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21922)
**ArXiv:** 2610.21922 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21922)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21922.pdf)]

**Authors:** Menghao Yin, Jiaju Yin, Xiyue Dong, ..., Yating Feng, Renjing Xu

**Abstract:** Many vision-language-action policies rely on action-labeled robot demonstrations and lack an explicit mechanism for predicting future visual states. These limitations hinder learning from action-free videos and can encourage overfitting to demonstration conditions, weakening generalization and robustness. We present TS-WAM, a lightweight Teacher-Student World-Action Model that addresses these challenges through self-supervised temporal learning and explicit future prediction. Its vlm-jepa framework learns from heterogeneous data, including robot demonstrations and action-free human videos, using an EMA target branch without requiring a separately trained external teacher. By coupling vlm-jepa with delta-lwm, TS-WAM learns to predict latent actions from the current observation and instruction and uses them to predict future visual features that guide action generation at inference. A unified end-to-end training architecture jointly optimizes temporal representation learning, visual dynamics, and robot control without a separate latent-action or world-model pretraining stage. The EMA target branch is removed at inference, keeping the deployed policy lightweight. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and SimplerEnv, together with real-world manipulation experiments on Franka Research 3 and RevoTron, demonstrate that TS-WAM achieves strong manipulation performance, generalization, and robustness.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 1: TS-WAM couples EMA-based temporal learning from robot demonstrations and action-free human videos with future-feature prediction that guides robotic action generation, evaluated on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, SimplerEnv, and real-world manipulation.

### 1002\. [VeriVLA: Adaptive Test-Time Verification for Flow-Based VLAs with Latent World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21985)
**ArXiv:** 2610.21985 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21985)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21985.pdf)]

**Authors:** Xueli Sun, Yuxiang Lu, Yantong Liu, ..., Zhe Liu, Hengshuang Zhao

**Abstract:** Flow-based Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies are inherently stochastic: different noise seeds can produce action chunks of varying quality from the same observation. At test time, however, the policy typically commits to a single sample without an explicit estimate of its quality. In fine-grained manipulation, executing a suboptimal action can irreversibly disrupt task progress and lead to failure. To exploit policy stochasticity, we introduce a training-free two-axis trigger that uses two initial samples to measure within-chunk temporal variation and cross-sample variance, detecting the critical states. Once triggered, a JEPA-based latent world model ranks candidate action chunks by predicting action-conditioned future latent states and the corresponding changes in task progress. By operating in latent space, the verifier avoids pixel reconstruction and enables efficient and more accurate ranking. Across three real-world tasks, $\textit{i.e.,}$ pen insertion, pipetting, and apple placement), our method raises the average completion score from 50.8% to 65.5%, while adding 326 ms for sampling and verification per triggered decision on a single RTX 4090. Consistent gains are also observed in simulation, where the average success rate rises from 66.4% to 73.6% in the clean setting of RoboTwin 2.0, from 62.8% to 69.6% in its randomized setting, and from 96.9% to 97.6% on LIBERO.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: VeriVLA uses a JEPA-based latent world model to rank candidate action chunks from flow-based VLA manipulation policies, improving average real-world completion scores from 50.8% to 65.5% across pen insertion, pipetting, and apple placement.

**Relevance:** 10

### 1003\. [REKI: Registering Knowledge in VLAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22178)
**ArXiv:** 2610.22178 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22178)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22178.pdf)]

**Authors:** Juliette Marrie, Paul Pacaud, Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Shizhe Chen, Moritz Böhle

**Abstract:** We introduce REKI, a 2.4B vision-language-action model (VLA) pretrained exclusively on public data that sets a new Pareto frontier between performance and model size: on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin Clean2Rand and RoboCasa365, it is outperformed only by models about twice its size. REKI consists of a vision-language model (VLM) followed by a flow-matching action expert, and is pretrained on a curated mixture of public robot, egocentric human and vision-language data. This memory improves success in simulation and on real robots, and makes oscillations between poses about four times rarer. REKI uses a simple model design and training paradigm, and is optimised to be efficient at training and inference. We release the full code across data curation, training, inference and evaluation, deployment, and all pretrained and finetuned model weights: https://github.com/kyutai-labs/reki.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 1: REKI combines a VLM with a flow-matching action expert and persistent register-token memory, improving manipulation success in simulation and on real robots while making pose oscillations about four times rarer.

### 1004\. [CoDA: Co-Evolution of Data Engines and Action Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22362)
**ArXiv:** 2610.22362 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22362)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22362.pdf)]

**Authors:** Tianjiao Yu, Zhilin Ou, Yifan Shen, ..., Fan Wang, Ismini Lourentzou

**Abstract:** Generative simulation can automate robot environment construction, but physically valid environments are not necessarily useful for training. The challenge is to identify environments that address a policy's failures and supervision that helps it overcome them. We introduce CoDA, a framework in which a simulation data engine and an action policy co-evolve through a shared heterogeneous memory of environment construction, policy failures, and learning progress. The memory compiles validated diagnoses from a vision-language critic into generation programs that recreate difficulties and training programs that help the policy overcome them. We further propose Text2potential to convert these diagnoses into staged potentials for action-chunk value learning, providing dense supervision for policy updates while preserving the task's optimal policy under standard shaping assumptions. Measured learning progress guides further generation and program refinement. Across four manipulation task families, CoDA improves out-of-distribution success from 5.3% after supervised fine-tuning to 27.6%, exceeding the strongest closed-loop baseline by 9.5 percentage points with the same generation budget. On LIBERO-Pro, 15 rounds of generation and training raise success from 25% to 84% under position perturbations and from 1% to 76% under instruction perturbations.

**Comment:** Criteria 1 and 7: CoDA combines a vision-language critic, shared memory, and feedback-driven environment generation to improve manipulation policies, raising LIBERO-Pro success under instruction perturbations from 1% to 76%.

### 1006\. [Spark-0: Scaling Spatially Aligned Tactile Pretraining for Robot Dexterous Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22342)
**ArXiv:** 2610.22342 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22342)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22342.pdf)]

**Authors:** Tianxing Chen, Wenwei Lin, Chuqiao Lyu, ..., Ping Luo, Wenbo Ding

**Abstract:** Generalist robot policies benefit from large-scale visual pretraining but often struggle with contact-rich dexterous manipulation, where force, compliance, and incipient slip are hard to observe from images. We present Spark-0, a spatially aligned vision-tactile-language-action (VTLA) model that uses touch in two complementary ways. During pretraining, image-space contact maps derived from tactile gloves, together with features from a frozen 3D geometry teacher, supervise the visual tokens; these objectives are removed at inference. The Website is available at: https://xsparkai.com/Spark-0.

### 1008\. [T2-z: Test-Time Optimization of Environment Latents for Robotic World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22364)
**ArXiv:** 2610.22364 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22364)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22364.pdf)]

**Authors:** Chenyuan Zhou, Yining Hong, Huang Huang, ..., Leonidas Guibas, Yejin Choi

**Abstract:** Predicting how the world responds to an action requires more than what can be seen: robots must account for environmental factors that are neither directly observable nor explicitly specified, from physical properties such as friction and mass to human-defined environmental rules. We introduce T2-Z for test-time optimization of environment latents, which augments a robotic world model with a learnable environment latent to condition world predictions. This compact latent captures underlying environmental variation without supervision of what environmental factors exist or what values they take. During training, T2-Z alternates between two stages: a world-model optimization stage that fixes the latents and learns general action-conditioned dynamics across environments; and an environment-latent optimization stage that freezes the world model and optimizes the conditioning latents, allowing latent structure to emerge purely through world prediction. At test time, the world model remains frozen and a newly initialized environment latent is optimized from one or more test-time interactions, adapting the model to previously unseen environmental configurations. Across 7 simulated settings and 4 real-world robotic tasks, T2-Z reduces object prediction error metric from 15.20 to 7.00 and raises action success from 33.73% to 72.96%. Its emergent latents capture structured representations of individual and compounded factors across diverse visual scenes. Ablations further show that T2-Z can efficiently infer environment latents from as little as a single test-time interaction trajectory.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: T2-Z adapts robotic world models through test-time optimization of unsupervised environment latents, raising action success from 33.73% to 72.96% across simulated and real-world robotic evaluations.

### 1011\. [MOSS: Mode-Oriented Source Shaping for Robot Flow Matching Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22193)
**ArXiv:** 2610.22193 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22193)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22193.pdf)]

**Authors:** Zirun Ma, Jindou Jia, Tuo An, ..., Jiahua Dong, Jianfei Yang

**Abstract:** In robotic manipulation, the same observation often admits multiple feasible actions. Generative policies such as diffusion and flow matching naturally capture such multimodal behaviors, yet which mode is executed is left to random sampling and cannot be actively controlled. We propose Mode-Oriented Source Shaping (MOSS), which turns mode selection into an explicit choice of where generation starts. We first reveal, both empirically and theoretically, that deterministic flow transport induces mode-dependent structure in the source space. Under ideal deterministic ODE transport, distinct behavioral modes have distinct source-space preimages, while practical approximation errors can blur their boundaries due to imbalanced data, limited model capacity, and few inference steps. Motivated by this source-space structure, MOSS constructs semantically aligned mode-specific Gaussian sources that encode mode identity in the initial noise. A modality-strength estimator predicts the local degree of multimodality, which adaptively controls the geometry of the mode-specific sources. It contracts them toward a shared Gaussian where behaviors are similar and separates them near behavioral branching points. During deployment, the desired behavior is selected at inference simply by sampling from the corresponding component. Across 6 simulated and 3 real-world manipulation tasks, MOSS steers to the target mode up to 100% of the time with high task success, recovers minority modes at over 90% guidance rate under a 10:90 imbalance dataset, and surpasses mode-conditioned flow policy by 43.3 points of guidance rate on real robots.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: MOSS introduces mode-specific Gaussian sources for controllable flow-matching robot manipulation policies, demonstrating up to 100% target-mode guidance in evaluations spanning six simulated and three real-world tasks.

### 1012\. [FastVLA: Task-Conditioned and Temporally Adaptive Visual Token Compression for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22146)
**ArXiv:** 2610.22146 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22146)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22146.pdf)]

**Authors:** Jiuyi Xu, Chengyuan Qian, Zhengzhong Tu, Yang Sui, Yangming Shi

**Abstract:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models present a promising paradigm for general-purpose robot manipulation, but closed-loop deployment is often limited by inference efficiency: at each timestep, high-resolution, often multi-view images are encoded into hundreds of visual tokens that dominate transformer computation and latency. Token compression via attention is a natural solution but is challenging for VLA control because (1) decision-critical tokens are determined by the task, not generic visual saliency, and (2) the token budget varies substantially across steps, so static metrics and budgets waste computation or discard control-critical tokens. We introduce FastVLA, a training-free, task-aware, and temporally adaptive visual token compression framework. At each step, FastVLA scores token importance via task-to-vision attention and adaptively sets the compression rate from the importance-distribution uniformity, measured by an inverse-Simpson metric with online sliding-window calibration. We evaluate FastVLA on OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and CogACT across LIBERO and SIMPLER, and on a physical Franka Research 3 robot. In simulation, it reduces LLM-backbone FLOPs by 35-59\% and action latency by 9-32\% (a 1.09$\times$-1.46$\times$ speedup) while maintaining task success, achieving a comparable or better efficiency-performance trade-off than existing plug-and-play token compression/pruning/reuse baselines. On the real robot, it cuts end-to-end latency by 20\% (1.25$\times$ speedup) at comparable success. Code is available at https://github.com/jiuyixu25/FastVLA.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 1: FastVLA uses task-conditioned attention and temporally adaptive token compression to accelerate VLA manipulation policies, reducing real-robot end-to-end latency by 20% while maintaining comparable task success.
