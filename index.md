### 1000\. [Tokens of Motion: Primitive Motion Learning for Human-Humanoid Skill Transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37330)
**ArXiv:** 2610.37330 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37330)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37330.pdf)]

**Authors:** Hanwen Ren, Meng Zheng, Wenzhe Cui, ..., Terrence Chen, Ziyan Wu

**Abstract:** Vision-language-action (VLA) models provide a promising paradigm for scalable robotic manipulation, yet their reliance on success-only behavioral cloning leaves them brittle; lacking corrective training signals, minor execution errors rapidly compound into unrecoverable, out-of-distribution failures. To address this limitation, we propose Adaptive Failure-Informed Learning (AFIL), an end-to-end framework that leverages failure trajectories as adaptive negative guidance for diffusion- and flow-based VLA policies. AFIL uses a pretrained VLA to generate failure rollouts online, avoiding the need for handcrafted failure-mode design or human-in-the-loop recovery. It then jointly trains Dual Action Generators (DAGs) for successful and failed behaviors while sharing a common vision-language backbone, enabling efficient failure-aware policy learning with limited parameter overhead. During sampling, the failure generator adaptively steers action generation away from failure-prone regions and toward more reliable success modes, with guidance strength determined by the per-diffusion-step distance between success and failure distributions. Experiments across in-domain and out-of-domain robotic manipulation tasks, covering both short- and long-horizon settings, show that AFIL consistently improves task success rates and robustness over existing VLA baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness, efficiency, and generality.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Adaptive Failure-Informed Learning uses online failure trajectories and dual action generators to guide diffusion- and flow-based VLA policies away from failure-prone actions, improving robotic manipulation success and robustness.

### 1001\. [PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381)
**ArXiv:** 2610.37381 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37381.pdf)]

**Authors:** Hanqing Wang, Zhaotian Cai, Ruichuan An, ..., Zenan Zhou, Youzheng Wu

**Abstract:** Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. The module learns through direct prediction of future RGB, depth, and optical-flow features, with auxiliary supervision through a frozen pretrained video world model. Training relies entirely on publicly available data, expanded through human-to-robot synthesis, subtask annotation, and embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) annotation. Across four simulation benchmarks, PDRVLA achieves success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin~2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv. Ablations show improved future prediction with world-model supervision and evaluate how subtask conditioning, future object-pose supervision, and ECoT training affect action success.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 1: PDRVLA uses frozen video-world-model supervision and future RGB, depth, and optical-flow feature prediction to learn physical dynamics representations for robotic action generation, achieving 99.3% success on LIBERO and 93.6% on RoboTwin 2.0.

### 1038\. [Smooth Action Chunking Via Movement-Primitive Representations for Imitation Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39451)

**Abstract:** Imitation learning has become a widely adopted approach for vision-based robot policies, owing to its conceptual simplicity, scalability, and ability to leverage large-scale datasets.
