### 1000\. [Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models to Stop Safely](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26239)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26239 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26239)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26239.pdf)]

**Authors:** Zheyuan Liu, Chuhan Wang, Jinduo Guo, ..., Ningshan Ma, Meng Jiang

**Abstract:** Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable robots to perform diverse tasks from visual observations and language instructions. While prior work has explored task suppression and refusal, stopping prohibited behavior once execution is underway and controlling the resulting physical response remain challenging. Our evaluation reveals that policies which suppress a task from its initial state can still complete it when taking over partway through execution. Even when the task remains unfinished, the robot may drop a held object, making non-completion an incomplete measure of safety. To address these challenges, we introduce State-Aware Fallback Imitation (SAFI) for tasks where holding provides an appropriate alternative response. SAFI comprises two stages, (1) fallback target construction, which assigns stationary arm commands and consistent gripper targets throughout prohibited-task demonstrations, and (2) joint policy adaptation, which learns these targets alongside the original actions of retained tasks. This supervision teaches an explicit alternative response at intermediate execution states while maintaining the robot's other skills. Extensive experiments with OpenVLA-OFT across LIBERO and BadLIBERO tasks evaluate prohibited completion, physical incidents, and retained-task performance. On the evaluated LIBERO tasks, SAFI reduces aggregate target success after takeover to 1.3\%, with no recorded physical incidents or action saturation, while preserving high retained-task success.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: State-Aware Fallback Imitation adapts OpenVLA-OFT to stop manipulation safely mid-task, reducing prohibited-task completion after takeover to 1.3% on the evaluated LIBERO tasks with no recorded physical incidents.

### 1001\. [TacInno: Teaching Vision-Language-Action Models the Contact Dynamics Beyond Vision and Action](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26405)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26405 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26405)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26405.pdf)]

**Authors:** Weile Li, Yunfan Lou, Muqing Shi, ..., Xiyu Zhu, Zhibo Pang

**Abstract:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have recently been equipped with tactile sensing to perceive visually subtle contact events in contact-rich manipulation. However, adding tactile input yields inconsistent gains, and some visuo-tactile VLA models rely little on tactile input, because much of the tactile change, such as the gel deformation during gripper closure, is predictable from visual observations and executed actions. To address this problem, we introduce TacInno, which isolates the contact dynamics that vision and executed actions cannot explain, termed tactile innovation, and uses tactile innovation only as a training target. Specifically, the TacInno tokenizer, conditioned on visual observations and executed actions, compresses the future tactile change into a discrete code, and the policy, TacInno-VLA, learns to predict the code, adding no module for the code at deployment. TacInno-VLA achieves average success rates of 87.5% across six simulation tasks and 70.5% across four real-world tasks, outperforming the strongest visuo-tactile baseline by 10.7 and 9.0 percentage points, respectively. Ablation studies validate the contributions of visual and action conditioning, multi-frame encoding, and auxiliary code prediction. Together, these results demonstrate the effectiveness of tactile innovation as a supervision signal for contact-rich manipulation.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: TacInno trains a vision-language-action policy to predict tactile changes not explained by vision and executed actions, improving success by 9 percentage points over the strongest visuo-tactile baseline across four real-world manipulation tasks.

### 1002\. [Awaken-HI: Scaling Humanoid Intelligence with Whole-Body Human Experience](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26420)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26420 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26420)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26420.pdf)]

**Authors:** EngineAI

**Abstract:** A central goal of humanoid robotics is for robots to perform the everyday work people do in homes and workplaces. Existing egocentric video datasets cover many of these activities but provide little synchronized whole-body motion data for learning loco-manipulation at scale. We build Awaken-Human, which pairs approximately 50,000 hours of egocentric stereo video with synchronized whole-body human motion and covers more than 100 skills. Building on this, we also introduce Awaken-HI (Humanoid Intelligence), a vision-language-action model for humanoid loco-manipulation that jointly predicts original human motion and retargeted robot actions through a shared action expert. We show that loco-manipulation skills transfer more effectively from people to humanoids when human trajectories directly supervise the shared action expert. Increasing human pretraining data reduces held-out robot action prediction error according to an empirical scaling law and raises the mean success rate on downstream real-robot tasks. After full-corpus pretraining and T800 adaptation, Awaken-HI achieves a mean success rate of 93.75% across eight individual tasks and outperforms GR00T N1.7 and $π_{0.5}$ in both individual and long-horizon evaluations. The same policy generalizes to objects unseen during robot post-training and to new pairings of familiar objects and destinations.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Awaken-HI trains a vision-language-action policy to predict human motion and retargeted robot actions through a shared action expert, reaching 93.75% mean success across eight real-robot loco-manipulation tasks.

### 1004\. [Risk-Controlled Test-Time Adaptation for Vision-Language-Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26438)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26438 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26438)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26438.pdf)]

**Authors:** Zixuan Liu, Hashem Haghbayan, Zizhan Zheng, Siavash Khajavi

**Abstract:** Retrieval-augmented vision-language-action (VLA) models enable efficient test-time adaptation by leveraging expert demonstrations as additional context without updating model parameters. However, even when retrieved demonstrations are relevant to the current task, the VLA may fail to effectively utilize the retrieved information, ultimately leading to task failure. To address this challenge, we propose R2-VLA, a risk-controlled retrieval-augmented VLA framework that enables reliable test-time adaptation by satisfying a user-specified task success rate requirement with minimal expert assistance. Specifically, R2-VLA learns an uncertainty predictor that estimates the uncertainty of the VLA's predicted actions given the retrieved demonstrations, guiding when to act autonomously or request expert assistance. We further introduce a task-level risk-control procedure that calibrates the uncertainty threshold to satisfy a user-specified task-failure risk requirement. Moreover, R2-VLA continually incorporates expert corrections into its retrieval memory to improve future adaptation while preserving the prescribed task-level risk guarantee. Experiments across three VLA backbones and four LIBERO benchmark suites demonstrate that R2-VLA improves average task success by 9.2--11.2 percentage points, achieving the desired target with fewer expert requests. These results highlight the effectiveness of connecting the usefulness of retrieved demonstrations with task-level risk control to enable successful robotic manipulation.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 1: R2-VLA uses action uncertainty to decide when to request expert help during robotic manipulation, improving average task success by 9.2–11.2 percentage points across four LIBERO benchmark suites.

### 1006\. [PGX: Physically Grounded Execution for Long-Horizon Humanoid Loco-Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26596)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26596 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26596)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26596.pdf)]

**Authors:** Kai Lu, Chenyang Ma, Ioannis Havoutis

**Abstract:** Robotic vision-language-action (VLA) models can execute diverse individual manipulation skills, yet they still struggle with long-horizon loco-manipulation tasks, where temporal structure, skills, and precision are tightly coupled within a monolithic model. We propose Physically Grounded Execution (PGX), a framework that offloads temporal and precision control to two classical training-free components, allowing the VLA to focus on stage-specific skill dynamics. PGX decomposes long-horizon loco-manipulation into three layers: (i) a predicate-based state machine governs task progression, using physical predicates to gate phase transitions, trigger recovery, and coordinate navigation and waiting. Shared predicate semantics connect offline demonstration segmentation, online execution, and stage-wise evaluation; (ii) VLA skills operate within active manipulation phases, with flat or stage-trained models concentrating on task-relevant actions; and (iii) world-coordinate locking (WCL) maintains spatial consistency through base station keeping, object-relative positioning, and world-anchored contact targets. We demonstrate the effectiveness of PGX with extensive experiments conducted on three simulated tasks and one real-world task using a Unitree G1 humanoid robot for long-horizon loco-manipulation, showing improvements in stage progression and task success rates. Project page is available at: https://kl-research.github.io/pgx

**Comment:** Criterion 1: PGX uses a predicate-based state machine to coordinate vision-language-action skills during long-horizon humanoid manipulation, improving task success in simulation and on a Unitree G1.

### 1009\. [One Prior, Two Dynamics: Repurposing Video Diffusion Transformers for Self-Supervised Latent Action Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26617 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26617)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26617.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yuxin Jiang, Wenqiang Sun, Jiaqi Li, ..., Ivor Tsang, Mike Zheng Shou

**Abstract:** Videos reveal how the world changes, but rarely the actions that cause those changes. Latent action learning recovers such supervision from visual transitions, yet often learns the underlying dynamics largely from scratch. We ask whether a pretrained video generator can instead provide the prior for both inverse and forward dynamics. We introduce O$^{2}$LaF, built on a simple principle: one prior, two dynamics. We initialize both models from the same pretrained video DiT and adapt them to their distinct roles: a feed-forward transition encoder with learnable action queries for inverse dynamics, and a causal, action-conditioned video generator for forward dynamics. By controlling information access and optimizing both dynamics end-to-end through a compact bottleneck, O$^{2}$LaF learns transferable actions from unlabeled videos. Across navigation and robotic manipulation, O$^{2}$LaF sets a new state of the art in few-shot action probing and enables stronger zero-shot action transfer across visual contexts and embodiments. Beyond generative video priors, we further extend our framework to predictive representation priors such as V-JEPA2, achieving competitive performance. Our findings suggest a broader paradigm for latent-action learning: repurposing pretrained video models to discover transferable actions and predict their consequences.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: O²LaF repurposes a pretrained video diffusion transformer as an inverse-dynamics encoder and an action-conditioned world model, learning latent actions from unlabeled video that improve few-shot action probing and zero-shot transfer in robotic manipulation.

### 1011\. [ViDecide: Teaching Video Diffusion Models to Reason and Plan Step by Step](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26389)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26389 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26389)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26389.pdf)]

**Authors:** Kaiwen Zhang, Yiming Gao, Zeqi Xiao, ..., Kun Gai, Xingang Pan

**Abstract:** Current leading video generation models can synthesize highly realistic videos, while still frequently violating physical and logical constraints in multi-step reasoning and planning, even after training with large-scale reasoning data or existing diffusion RL methods. We attribute these failures to the conventional video training paradigm, which treats a solution as a fixed-length clip rather than a chain of decisions. We present ViDecide, a decision-centric framework that trains video diffusion models to predict state transitions causally and decide when to stop, with a decision-focused training schedule. We further propose Corrective Fine-tuning (CFT), which corrects failed decisions by mapping their noise to the model's own successful continuations, achieving stable improvement without policy-gradient optimization. Across abstract games, planning tasks, and a new benchmark of multi-step robot planning, ViDecide achieves an average strict success rate of 83.8%, far above 23.3% for standard fine-tuning and 67.8% for the strongest prior method, and generalizes to larger puzzles and unseen layouts. Our framework therefore unlocks greater potential for video generation models as reliable reasoners and planners in the visual world. Project page: https://kevin-thu.github.io/ViDecide.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: ViDecide trains a video diffusion model to predict state transitions step by step and decide when to stop, and evaluates it on a new multi-step robot planning benchmark.

### 1014\. [AgentRoboCritic: Understanding the Capability of Frontier Agents on Direct Robot Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26613)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26613 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26613)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26613.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yiyang Du, Yuxuan Kuang, Qitao Zhao, ..., Liu Ren, Chenyan Xiong

**Abstract:** Frontier multimodal language agents such as GPT-6 Astra have demonstrated strong capabilities in controlling robots directly via low-level numerical actions without high-level primitives. However, existing benchmarks report only episode-level success rates, which show neither how these agents succeed nor where they fail. In this paper, we present a systematic action-level analysis of frontier agents on direct robot control across 12 manipulation benchmarks. We introduce AgentRoboCritic, an intent-grounded critique framework in which the policy agent states a local intent alongside each action, and a critic evaluates the action's outcome and failure mode against this intent. Our action-level analysis demonstrates the promising capabilities of frontier agents on direct robot control, including closed-loop recovery from local execution failures, reliable semantic grounding across diverse scenes, and strong zero-shot manipulation without task-specific adaptation. It also identifies their current limitations, including task-relevant physical execution bottlenecks such as grasping and alignment, and uneven recovery across failure modes. Finally, we validate our critic on a 400-action human-annotated benchmark and release a dataset of 12,844 annotated actions alongside a fine-tuned open-source critic, Muse-Glimmer-30B, whose failure-mode accuracy rises from 32.58% to 74.24%, enabling low-cost action-level analysis.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: AgentRoboCritic evaluates multimodal language agents that ground local intents in low-level robot actions, documenting zero-shot manipulation and closed-loop recovery from execution failures across 12 manipulation benchmarks.

### 1017\. [Beyond the Sink Interpretation: Radial Attention in Flow-Based VLA Policies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26385)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26385 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26385)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26385.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yuxi Zheng, Yueqian Lin, Yudong Liu, ..., Hai Helen Li, Yiran Chen

**Abstract:** Prior work calls the high attention on the first and last action tokens of a flow-based VLA policy a radial action sink, and uses these tokens to set the execution horizon. In language models, a sink is a token with high incoming attention, a small value, and little effect on the output. We test whether that profile holds, not the horizon rule, in one $π_{0.5}$ checkpoint on five RoboCasa tasks. The two endpoints receive 5.4% of action-key mass, 1.4 percentage points above their uniform share, and rotary positional encoding produces this excess: directly in shallow layers and through upstream action-token RoPE deeper in the network. The endpoints do not fit the sink profile. Their value norms stay near the action-token mean in all 18 layers. On 250 paired episodes, masking them or zeroing their values changes success by less than 4 points, and replacing their value directions with those of another observation lowers it by 3.2 to 7.2 points across three donors, whereas the same replacement on two non-endpoint tokens in the executed prefix lowers it by 28.4 points. The token that does fit the sink profile on mass and value norm is the prefix BOS; on this checkpoint, zeroing its value raises success in two paired runs, by +13.2 points (95% CI [+7.0, +19.4]) and +6.0 points ([+0.2, +11.8]). Inside the action chunk, a relative-offset routing profile estimated on other tasks keeps 75% of action-token spread and keeps closed-loop success on three held-out tasks (42.7% against a 38.7% baseline), while uniform or shuffled routing drops success to 3-4%. High attention mass alone does not tell what a token does.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Closed-loop RoboCasa experiments show that relative-offset attention routing preserves the flow-based π₀.₅ vision-language-action policy’s manipulation success on three held-out tasks, while uniform or shuffled routing drops success to 3–4%.

### 5007\. [Direct3D-V3: Latent-Free Diffusion Transformers in the Native Voxel Domain](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26511)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26511 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26511)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26511.pdf)]

**Authors:** Shuang Wu, Jiachen Qian, Youtian Lin, ..., Yao Yao, Xun Cao

**Abstract:** Native 3D generation has largely been built around latent diffusion, with recent advances concentrating on stronger variational autoencoders (VAE) and increasingly elaborate latent representations, from vecset and triplanes to structured sparse voxels. Despite their architectural differences, these methods all share the same premise: geometry is first compressed into a latent space and only then modeled by the generator. We take a different route and propose Direct3D-V3, a latent-free diffusion transformer (DiT) framework that discards the VAE altogether and operates in the native voxel domain, directly modeling raw SDF voxel grids during both training and sampling. To make this direct voxel formulation geometrically reliable, we introduce an implicit manifold regularization over neighboring voxels to suppress non-watertight surfaces, and convert predicted SDF grids into meshes via Marching Cubes so that rendered normal losses can supervise the model directly from its predicted SDF. To improve alignment between the conditioning image and generated geometry, Direct3D-V3 also predicts the camera parameters of the conditioning image as an auxiliary task, encouraging the DiT to learn the mapping from image observations to 3D shape. This latent-free design removes the reconstruction bottleneck, brings training under an objective in the native voxel domain, and yields meshes with substantially richer local detail. The predicted camera and native SDF voxel output further make Direct3D-V3 extensible to generative reconstruction, where diffusion is interleaved with normal-guided refinement to improve alignment with the observed view while the generative prior completes unseen regions. Project page: https://dreamtechai.github.io/Direct3D-V3.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 5: Direct3D-V3 replaces latent diffusion with a transformer that generates signed-distance voxel grids directly, using manifold regularization and rendered-normal supervision to produce meshes with richer local detail.

### 6013\. [SGAR: Feed-Forward 3D Reconstruction via Sparse Geometry-Guided Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26493)

**Authors:** Aoxiang Fan, Maria Pilligua, Corentin Dumery, Oliver Heinimann, Pascal Fua

**Abstract:** Feed-forward models have been very successful in 3D reconstruction with ad hoc attention designs that relate all view patches densely. However, the cost of this dense global attention grows quadratically with the number of views, preventing the model from applying to larger-scale reconstruction settings. In this paper, we argue that most dense attention computations are unnecessary from a classical structure-from-motion perspective: 3D structures stem from geometrically matching regions in 2D. We bring this principle to learning-based feed-forward reconstruction with our Sparse Geometric Attention Reconstruction (SGAR) method. In SGAR, each patch token attends to only a small set of patch tokens from other views, chosen by a lightweight learned key selection module trained to predict cross-view 3D proximity of tokens. SGAR is a general method that can be applied to different feed-forward models by fine-tuning. In our experiments, we show that SGAR is the fastest among both state-of-the-art dense and sparse methods and the most accurate sparse one. It runs up to 12-times faster than dense attention at 1,000 views with stable pose accuracy where existing sparse methods fail, and shows the best performance in reconstructing more complete geometries on the same time budget.

### 6015\. [OffBeat: Multicoset Sampling for 4D Capture Beyond the Camera Frame Rate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26347)
**ArXiv:** 2611.26347 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26347)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26347.pdf)]

**Authors:** Chi-Wei Lee, An-Kai Chen, Cheng-Han Chiang, Yi-Chun Chang, Po-Chih Kuo

**Abstract:** For 4D reconstruction, a synchronized multi-camera rig records all views at the same timestamps and misses the motion between them. Staggered triggers spread the same frames over more timestamps. However, when the cameras along each row fire one after another, as in 4DSloMo's schedule on the N3DV rig, the row acts as one moving camera that confounds depth with motion. We introduce OffBeat, which models a staggered rig as a spatially distributed multicoset sampler: shifted copies of one sampling grid. From the calibration alone, OffBeat derives camera groups that fire together and their firing order. Since one group records each timestamp, a short-lived primitive can fit an error its cameras share, such as a color offset, and flicker in novel views. On Spacetime Gaussians, a minimum lifetime approximates this bound, and a closed-form smoothed box of that length satisfies it exactly, with less flicker than a Gaussian in its place at nearly equal PSNR. In simulated staggered capture on N3DV, OffBeat outperforms the full 4DSloMo pipeline with video diffusion on every test frame, by 3.04 dB in mean dynamic-region PSNR. Between the first and last synchronized frame, it outperforms the best synchronized method of each scene, with or without frame interpolation, by 1.98 dB in mean dynamic-region PSNR, while synchronized capture is better at its own frames. On PanopticSports, in this frame range, OffBeat is comparable in PSNR to synchronized capture with interpolation. On every N3DV scene, replacing 4DSloMo's schedule with ours raises the dynamic-region PSNR of Spacetime Gaussians, 4DSloMo without diffusion, and our reconstruction. Multi-camera rigs could follow faster motion at the same frame count by changing only when each camera fires.

**Comment:** Criterion 6: OffBeat reconstructs dynamic 3D scenes with Spacetime Gaussians from staggered views, with only one camera group observing each timestamp, and improves mean dynamic-region PSNR by 3.04 dB over 4DSloMo on N3DV.

### 7005\. [Mind2Act World: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Embodied Mind, Physical Action, and Mind-Action Composition in Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26444)
