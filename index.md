### 1001\. [GPWM: Modeling the World as 3D Particle Motion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08338)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08338 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08338)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08338.pdf)]

**Authors:** Robert Ren, Matthew Bronars, He Zhu, ..., Pavel Tokmakov, Katerina Fragkiadaki

**Abstract:** We introduce GPWM, a Generative Particle World Model that predicts how 3D scenes evolve by generating the motion of particles and rigid bodies over time. Unlike video world models that generate scene appearance, GPWM operates directly in 3D physical space. A single representation and architecture cover rigid, deformable, cloth, and granular materials, in both passive physics and robotic interaction. GPWM is an object-aware multi-resolution transformer that combines flow matching with diffusion forcing to generate coherent physical trajectories far beyond its training horizon. We train and evaluate GPWM on ParticleWorlds, a 25.5-million-frame corpus spanning diverse geometries, materials, interactions, and robot embodiments. GPWM outperforms the best prior learned-dynamics baseline in each of six physical domains, reducing prediction error by 31% on average. Breaking from the domain-specific setting of prior methods, a single Unified GPWM models these heterogeneous regimes with one set of weights, largely outperforming per-domain baselines and even surpassing our own specialist models in two domains. GPWM further generalizes to unseen geometries, physical properties, object counts, and rollout horizons. Beyond prediction, GPWM enables contact-rich planning by learning a generative prior over joint actuator-object motion. Instead of searching over unconstrained actions, planning searches the model's latent noise for interactions that achieve a desired object-space goal. With the same world model, noise-space planning substantially outperforms action-space search on pushing and picking, where complex contact sequences are hard to discover. Executed open-loop in simulation, the resulting plans achieve goal-directed manipulation without reference action trajectories or goal-specific policies.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: GPWM combines flow matching and diffusion forcing in a 3D particle world model, enabling latent-noise-space planning that substantially outperforms action-space search for simulated robotic pushing and picking without goal-specific policies.

### 1002\. [OopsieRecover: Learning Transferable VLA Corrections from Semantic Interventions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08462)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08462 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08462)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08462.pdf)]

**Authors:** Arpit Bahety, Skand Peri, Sriniket Ambatipudi, ..., Masha Itkina, Roberto Martín-Martín

**Abstract:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform a wide range of robotic manipulation tasks, but often fail during deployment. Teaching them to recover from these failures requires substantial corrective data: existing approaches learn to recover from failures by directly associating corrective actions with the observations in which they were demonstrated. For instance, DAgger-like fine-tuning can require tens to hundreds of interactions, yet the resulting policy may still fail to generalize the learned corrections beyond the demonstrated scenarios. We hypothesize that semantics can capture the underlying reasons for failures and their corrections, enabling policies to learn from only a handful of corrective demonstrations and generalize those corrections to novel objects, and scene variations. We propose OopsieRecover, a framework that uses abduction to infer the intent behind a small set of corrective demonstrations and encodes the inferred knowledge as dense spatial representations and conditional costs. These costs capture when a behavior is undesirable and steer the policy toward more desirable action regions. We experiment across 6 tasks and variations in object poses, scenes, and related tasks, and find that only a handful of corrective demonstrations can enable a VLA to overcome failures across a broad range of novel situations, without requiring new corrective data for each variation. Please refer to https://robin-lab.cs.utexas.edu/oopsierecover for videos.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: OopsieRecover improves VLA-based robotic manipulation by abductively inferring corrective intent from a handful of demonstrations and encoding it as dense spatial representations and conditional costs, demonstrating transferable failure recovery across six tasks and novel object-pose and scene variations.

### 1003\. [SLA: Scaling Spatial-Language-Action Model via Continual Pre-training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08373)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08373 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08373)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08373.pdf)]

**Authors:** Fuhao Li, Wenxuan Song, Qizhou Huang, ..., Shunbo Zhou, Haoang Li

**Abstract:** Reliable robotic manipulation requires both semantic understanding and precise control in three-dimensional space. However, vision-language-action (VLA) foundations largely inherit visual representations from 2D vision-language pre-training, while spatial supervision is often introduced only during task-specific adaptation. Developing spatial understanding as a transferable foundation-model capability therefore calls for learning from broader robot experience. We present SLA, a Spatial-Language-Action Model that scales implicit spatial alignment to continual pre-training and maintains it during downstream post-training. Initialized from a pretrained pi05 policy, SLA jointly optimizes action prediction and alignment between intermediate visual embeddings and spatial representations from a frozen 3D perception model. We curate and unify heterogeneous robot datasets to support spatial learning across diverse embodiments, tasks, and scenes. To accommodate this diversity, we introduce feature-dependent gates that adaptively aggregate visual embeddings from multiple VLA layers for spatial supervision. The teacher further exploits partially available depth and camera calibration alongside RGB observations, enabling effective use of heterogeneous geometric annotations. Probabilistic state dropout encourages reliance on visual cues and mitigates overfitting to demonstration trajectories. All auxiliary components are used only during training, allowing direct reuse of pretrained VLA weights and preserving the original inference architecture.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: SLA improves a vision-language-action manipulation policy through continual pre-training that aligns intermediate visual embeddings with a frozen 3D perception teacher using feature-dependent gates, while preserving the original inference architecture.

### 1004\. [T3R: Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement Towards Efficient and Robust VLA Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08413)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08413 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08413)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08413.pdf)]

**Authors:** Mohammed Hassan, Zhenyang Chen, Zheng Wang, ..., Lin, Chaojian Li

**Abstract:** Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable end-to-end robot control, but inference is increasingly expensive. Each decision processes hundreds of visual tokens, often across multiple cameras, leading to substantial computation while allowing irrelevant background content to distract action prediction under distribution shift. We argue that these issues stem from two complementary forms of redundancy: many visual tokens are spatially irrelevant, while attention over the remaining tokens is often misallocated. Based on this observation, we propose T3R, a Training-Free Two-Stage Token Refinement framework. In the first stage, segmentation-guided token pruning removes irrelevant visual patches to reduce computation and eliminate distracting context. In the second stage, saliency-guided attention biasing reallocates attention toward action-critical visual regions and language instructions within the retained tokens. Together, pruning produces a cleaner candidate set, while attention biasing further emphasizes the most decision-relevant evidence, yielding a better accuracy-efficiency frontier than either stage alone. On LIBERO-Spatial, T3R removes 85% of scene-camera visual tokens while essentially matching the unpruned success rate, and improves success by up to 7 percentage points under out-of-distribution object-placement perturbations. The timing comparison attributes 7.8 ms per inference call to pruning and attention-bias application. As input resolution increases, LLM-forward profiling shows up to a 3.3x speedup over the unpruned baseline. Evaluations across LIBERO, SIMPLER, and RoboTwin with three VLA backbones show the strongest accuracy-efficiency benefits in multi-camera settings. These results demonstrate that training-free token refinement is a practical approach to efficient and robust VLA inference.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: T3R applies segmentation-guided token pruning and saliency-guided attention biasing to robot VLAs, removing 85% of scene-camera visual tokens on LIBERO-Spatial while essentially preserving task success.

### 1005\. [VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08449 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08449.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yu-Cheng Chou, Shihao Wang, Jin Huang, ..., Jan Kautz, Zhiding Yu

**Abstract:** Models for physical AI tasks, such as robotics and autonomous driving, need not reason solely in language. They can use visual plans--points, bounding boxes, and trajectories--to represent spatial goals and anticipate motion. However, autoregressive vision-language models generate these plans one coordinate token at a time, making richer predictions increasingly costly. We introduce VFlash, a method for fast, accurate visual planning through Flash Primitive Decoding. VFlash exploits the known structure of visual primitives to predict their coordinates jointly, using a lightweight drafter that generates an entire primitive in one forward pass. The layout adapts to the primitive's type, dimensionality, and length, supporting compact boxes and long, variable-length trajectories. Crucially, spatial accuracy need not require reproducing an autoregressive model's exact token sequence. VFlash therefore trains on annotated primitives and directly accepts structurally valid predictions without target-token agreement. Across the evaluated 2D and 3D robot trajectory tasks, VFlash consistently reduces prediction errors relative to autoregressive decoding. Preliminary single-rollout measurements indicate approximately 25x faster generation for 3D point motion forecasting. A world action model conditioned on VFlash plans achieves 70.2% average success across four MolmoSpaces task suites, compared with 62.6% for the baseline.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: VFlash jointly decodes visual-plan primitives for VLM-based robotic planning, with its plans raising a world action model's average success across four MolmoSpaces task suites from 62.6% to 70.2%.

### 1007\. [La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08156)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08156 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08156)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08156.pdf)]

**Authors:** Mateo Clémente, George Z. Xue, Amin Abyaneh, ..., Zhanguang Zhang, Yingxue Zhang

**Abstract:** World-action models adapt pretrained video generators, and vision-language-action models build on pretrained vision-language backbones; both tie policy architecture and inference cost to large transformers designed for generation rather than control. It remains unclear how much these generation-oriented choices affect control. In this work, we introduce Latent Action JEPA (La-JEPA), a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder. Following Fast-WAM, La-JEPA learns to predict future features jointly with actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, La-JEPA achieves state-of-the-art performance in clean and randomized RoboTwin 2.0 scenes and on LIBERO-Plus with correct instructions, while using far fewer parameters than the strongest competing policies. It runs substantially faster than Fast-WAM at comparable performance and outperforms the closest JEPA-based methods, LeapBot-WA and JEPA-WAM, on LIBERO-Plus and RoboTwin 2.0 without their pretrained policy backbones. On four real-robot tasks, its average success is comparable to pi0.5 and above non-embodied-pretrained baselines. Through a design study of the choices behind La-JEPA, we show that JEPA features outperform video VAE latents, most clearly out of distribution, and that training-time future prediction choices significantly impact the policy even when future prediction is skipped at inference. We correct the LIBERO-Plus instructions, as the officially released evaluator corrupts them for most variants, and re-evaluate several strong open baselines. Without a generative pretrained policy backbone, La-JEPA is better and faster than far larger policies, suggesting that robot foundation models may benefit from architectures designed for control rather than inherited from generation.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: La-JEPA introduces a compact world-action model that jointly predicts future V-JEPA 2.1 features and manipulation actions during training, achieving state-of-the-art performance among policies without embodied pretraining on RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO-Plus with corrected instructions.

### 1009\. [Failure Prediction and Classification in Vision-Language-Action Models Via Calibrated Latent Probing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08425)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08425 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08425)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08425.pdf)]

**Authors:** Michal P. Podolinsky, Yunhao Yang, Daniel Little, ..., Matthias Scheutz, Ufuk Topcu

**Abstract:** Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) enable general-purpose embodied intelligence, but their performance is brittle under distribution shift, where unanticipated failures can damage equipment or endanger people. Deploying VLAs in real-world systems therefore requires a runtime monitor that is timely, calibrated, and informative while being efficient enough to run on the edge. Existing methods fail to fulfill these requirements. We present VLA-Tug, a lightweight probe over the latent layers of a VLA backbone that improves early failure detection and bounds the missed-detection and false-alarm rates, with minimal inference overhead. We show that supervising on the pre-failure segment of a trajectory increases early failure detection at matched false-positive rates by up to 30% over the strongest learned baseline. Under shift to unseen tasks, the bounds on missed-detection and false-alarm rates remain valid in up to 86% of runs, versus at most 21% for baselines. Unlike existing methods, VLA-Tug predicts the failure mode to enable corrective actions, raising top-3 accuracy on unseen tasks by up to 44% over a majority baseline. VLA-Tug enables real-time monitoring where the well-timed and informative probe supports lightweight steering that improves task success. We release a dataset of 3,180 annotated manipulation episodes to support future work on latent failure detection in VLAs at https://vla-tug.github.io.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: VLA-Tug probes VLA latent states for calibrated failure detection and failure-mode prediction during manipulation, supporting lightweight runtime steering that improves task success.

### 6006\. [SplatAnything: Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-forward 3DGS across Arbitrary Scenes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08264)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08264 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08264)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08264.pdf)]

**Authors:** Hao Yu, Zhenwei Wang, Yongzhen Hu, ..., Chunchao Guo, Sida Peng

**Abstract:** We identify a key limitation of existing feed-forward 3DGS methods: Gaussian attributes are typically predicted in a radial-distance-normalized space that canonicalizes a global radial statistic of the reconstructed geometry, while the spacing between neighboring Gaussian centers can still vary substantially across scene scales and camera configurations. This mismatch induces systematic shifts in Gaussian-scale targets, leading to ambiguous supervision. To address this, we propose SplatAnything with Gaussian Spacing Normalization (GSNorm), which canonicalizes a robust scene-level statistic of Gaussian spacing to provide a more consistent spatial reference for Gaussian prediction. To rigorously assess our method, we further establish a systematic benchmark spanning diverse datasets, input sparsity levels, and challenging extrapolated viewpoints. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SplatAnything achieves state-of-the-art performance, with particularly strong gains under substantial variations in scene scale and viewpoint.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 6 by introducing Gaussian Spacing Normalization for feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction, with state-of-the-art results on a benchmark spanning diverse datasets, input sparsity levels, and challenging extrapolated viewpoints.

### 6008\. [InstantHand: Feed-Forward 3D Gaussian Splatting for Animatable Hand Avatar Reconstruction from a Single Image](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08330)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08330 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08330)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08330.pdf)]

**Authors:** Chanho Kim, Muhammad Salman Ali, Kyeonghwan Gwak, ..., Jeongwan On, Seungryul Baek

**Abstract:** Hand avatar reconstruction typically relies on multi-view capture or per-subject optimization, limiting its scalability. We introduce InstantHand, a feed-forward framework that constructs an animatable 3D Gaussian hand avatar from a single image using off-the-shelf MANO, camera, and mask estimates, without test-time optimization. Image-averaged losses provide insufficient supervision for alpha leakage, which thickens thin finger contours. To address this issue, we propose a Soft Alpha Band regularizer that concentrates supervision on silhouette boundaries. Furthermore, colors regressed from image features can drift toward a learned prior even in observed regions. Our Reliability-gated Appearance Transfer preserves observed appearance by transferring source colors according to each Gaussian's rendering contribution, while completing unobserved regions using the learned prior conditioned on global source identity. InstantHand constructs an avatar in 2.84 seconds and renders at 161 FPS, matching per-subject-optimized baselines on InterHand2.6M and remaining competitive in zero-shot evaluation on HanCo.

**Comment:** Criterion 6: InstantHand reconstructs an animatable 3D Gaussian hand avatar from a single image without test-time optimization, using Soft Alpha Band regularization and Reliability-gated Appearance Transfer to match per-subject-optimized baselines on InterHand2.6M.

### 6010\. [Hierarchical Incremental Beta Splatting for Online Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08288)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08288 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08288)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08288.pdf)]

**Authors:** Linus Franke

**Abstract:** Online 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstructs a scene during capture: each keyframe spawns primitives once and refines them for only a few dozen iterations, leaving little chance to fix poor initialization or missing detail. Gaussians are also inherently soft, needing many overlaps for solid surfaces. We present Hierarchical Incremental Beta Splatting (HIBS), which brings beta kernels to incremental reconstruction. Their shape parameter interpolates between a soft, Gaussian-like profile and a flat-topped, near-opaque surfel, acting as a confidence dial for geometry. Our confidence-aware spawning uses geometric observability and multi-view depth agreement to initialize both opacity and shape, producing solid primitives where geometry is verifiable and soft, correctable primitives where it is not. As online densification, error-driven relocation recycles stale primitives into photometric-error hotspots. Bounded beta kernel support enables exact tile culling and faster rasterization, and a closed-form, shape-preserving merging formulation provides a level-of-detail (LOD) hierarchy for fast optimization and real-time rendering. On Mip-NeRF 360 in the on-the-fly protocol, HIBS improves PSNR by up to 0.8 dB while reducing optimization time by up to 40%. Project page: https://linus-franke.com/hibs/

**Comment:** Criterion 6: HIBS advances online splatting reconstruction with confidence-aware beta kernels, error-driven primitive relocation, and a shape-preserving LOD hierarchy, improving PSNR by up to 0.8 dB while reducing optimization time by up to 40% on Mip-NeRF 360.

### 6011\. [MOMASPLAT: Dynamic Novel-View Demonstration Augmentation for Mobile Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08372)
**ArXiv:** 2611.08372 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08372)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08372.pdf)]

**Authors:** Sateesh Kumar, Arnav Balaji, Arpit Bahety, ..., Georgios Pavlakos, Roberto Martín-Martín

**Abstract:** Visuomotor policies trained through imitation learning are highly sensitive to changes in camera viewpoint. This is particularly problematic for mobile manipulation (MoMa), where robots rarely execute manipulation from the exact same base pose used during demonstration collection due to navigation uncertainty, localization drift, and environmental constraints. Recent approaches use novel-view synthesis (NVS) to augment demonstrations with unseen viewpoints, but they assume static scenes and therefore fail to re-render the manipulated object during the post-contact interaction phase, leaving the most critical parts of the demonstration unaugmented. We present MOMASPLAT, a novel-view demonstration augmentation framework for mobile manipulation that synthesizes full manipulation trajectories from unseen robot base poses, including dynamic object interactions. Our key insight is that standard MoMa data collection naturally provides the multiview observations needed to reconstruct both the static scene and the manipulated object in 3D. MOMASPLAT combines 3D Gaussian Splatting, feed-forward 3D foundation models, and kinematics-based action retargeting to separately reconstruct the scene and manipulated object, render them from novel viewpoints, and generate geometrically consistent synthetic demonstrations paired with transformed actions. We evaluate MOMASPLAT on five real-world mobile manipulation tasks involving both rigid and articulated objects across three scenes, and validate the fidelity of its synthesized demonstrations against ground truth in simulation. MOMASPLAT substantially improves robustness to unseen base poses, more than doubling the average success rate of the strongest baseline and maintaining non-trivial success at large base displacements where all baselines fail, while reducing the need for largescale real-world data collection.

**Comment:** Criterion 6: MOMASPLAT combines 3D Gaussian Splatting and feed-forward 3D foundation models to reconstruct scenes and manipulated objects for dynamic novel-view demonstration augmentation, more than doubling the strongest baseline’s average success rate across five real-world mobile-manipulation tasks.

**ArXiv:** 2611.08209 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08209)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08209.pdf)]

**Authors:** Satvik Sharma, Hengyuan Hu, Mihika Kamat, Dorsa Sadigh, Jeannette Bohg

**Abstract:** Improvements in multi-modal capabilities have enabled agents to act directly as robot policies, a setting we refer to as agentic robot control (ARC). We introduce RoboARCade, a benchmark of long-horizon manipulation tasks across three embodiments designed to test in-context adaptation, memory, and reasoning for precise control. The tasks build on existing open-source benchmarks, which we make harder by widening the range of initial conditions. RoboARCade is easy to use and extend: all tasks run on a single simulation backend, MuJoCo, each task is a self-contained data pack, and agents connect through interchangeable interfaces, so new tasks, agents, and interfaces can be added independently. The benchmark separates leading agents: GPT-6 Astra achieves 49.8% compared with 43.6% for Claude Fable 5.1, leaving substantial room for improvement. Comparing two interfaces, we find that a richer visual interface adds only 1.5 to 6.8 points at 1.7 to 2.3 times the cost. Failures stem mostly from choosing a poor manipulation strategy or executing a good one imprecisely, rarely from misunderstanding the scene, and agents incorrectly claim success on 24% to 39% of attempted subtasks. Agents can also improve without human input: one successful demonstration in context raises Astra's score from 53.5% to 73.0% on a five-task subset, and a prompt the agent writes and revises itself reaches 72.0%. Code, videos and full traces are available at roboarcade.org.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 7 by introducing RoboARCade, a MuJoCo benchmark of long-horizon manipulation across three embodiments, where one in-context demonstration improves agent performance from 53.5% to 73.0% on a five-task subset.
