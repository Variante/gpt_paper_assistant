### 1001\. [ObjDream: Guiding Vision-Language-Action Models with Object-Level Understanding and Future State Prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20223)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20223 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20223)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20223.pdf)]

**Authors:** Huailiang Ma, Yuxuan Li, Ziyi Zhao, ..., Cewu Lu, Jianhua Sun

**Abstract:** Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as a powerful paradigm for robotic manipulation, enabling robots to generate actions from visual observations and language instructions. However, most existing VLA models still operate on image-level visual representations inherited from pretrained vision-language backbones, while lacking fine-grained object understanding for manipulation. As a result, their robustness can degrade under out-of-distribution object layouts, clutter, and background variations. To address these limitations, we introduce ObjDream, an object-centric VLA framework centered on object-level understanding and future object state prediction. ObjDream uses a spatio-temporal object encoder to organize visual observations into temporal object slots, where persistent object identity and dynamic object state are represented through separate channels. These structured object representations are inserted into the token sequence of the vision-language model (VLM), where identity-guided routing modulates VLM attention to the corresponding object tokens. In addition, an action-conditioned object dynamics module predicts compact future object states relevant to manipulation during training, explicitly supervising object state evolution under robot actions without requiring future prediction at inference. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, and RoboTwin 2.0 show that ObjDream improves generalization under diverse distribution shifts and scene perturbations. Real-world robot experiments further demonstrate that ObjDream transfers to physical manipulation settings with novel object arrangements and background changes.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: ObjDream integrates temporal object slots, identity-guided VLM attention, and action-conditioned future-state prediction into a robotic manipulation VLA, improving generalization on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world robots.

### 1002\. [Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning with Human Interventions for Precise Robotic Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20504)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20504 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20504)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20504.pdf)]

**Authors:** Xiaoman Li, Zhiqiang Yang, Jiayu Hu, ..., Chunhua Shen, Chenjia Bai

**Abstract:** Long-horizon precise manipulation produces mixed-quality data for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models: even successful demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts may contain detours, hesitations, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. The key challenge is to identify which action chunks actually advance the task, rather than uniformly cloning or discarding imperfect trajectories. We propose Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning (GR-BC), an offline iterative post-training framework that builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over demonstrations and iteratively collected rollouts, including both successful and failed episodes with human interventions. Reverse graph search computes minimal remaining steps to task success within the dataset support, and each action chunk is weighted by how much its execution reduces this potential. This consequence-based weighting reuses recoverable segments from failed trajectories, leverages interventions as recovery paths, and down-weights locally regressive chunks even inside successful trajectories. New rollouts are incorporated by appending them to the graph and rerunning search, without training a reward model, action-conditioned critic, or progress predictor. Across four precise manipulation tasks and three VLA backbones, GR-BC improves single-round supervised fine-tuning and reaches 91% success after a few offline iterations, without trajectory comparisons or progress labels. More details and videos are available at: https://gr-bc.github.io

**Comment:** Criterion 1: GR-BC post-trains vision-language-action models using goal-reachability graphs to reweight demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts, reaching 91% success on precise robotic manipulation after a few offline iterations.

### 1003\. [Physical Time as a Missing Dimension of Robot Policy Memory Dynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20524)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20524 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20524)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20524.pdf)]

**Authors:** Yushen Liang, Yue Peng, Baosheng Jin, ..., Xinqi Liu, Shenji Wan

**Abstract:** Existing robot policy memories typically evolve with observation sequences, implicitly treating each update as the same amount of physical time. Yet identical observation histories can span different durations and require different actions, exposing physical time as a missing dimension of policy memory. We introduce Temporal Flow Policy (TFP), which learns memory dynamics to retain past events and evolve the memory state over measured time intervals. Observations and prior memory determine the content and time scales of each transition; elapsed time determines how far the state advances. TFP conditions diffusion and flow-matching policies through this memory state and is trained with their native imitation objectives. We evaluate TFP with Diffusion Policy and $π_{0.5}$ through controlled comparisons of memory designs. These experiments show that successful timing depends on both measured intervals and retained history. TFP achieves higher average success than the recurrent and state-space memories we equip with the same intervals and than a variant that feeds the interval to a learned gate. Memory readouts recover the time since an event and motion information after occlusion. Changing the supplied intervals systematically delays or advances actions, linking time inputs to behavior. Real-robot tasks requiring timing or memory, together with LIBERO evaluations, demonstrate benefits in physical and general manipulation. These results suggest viewing robot memory not as stored observation history, but as a state that retains past events and evolves with physical time.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Temporal Flow Policy conditions diffusion and flow-matching manipulation policies on memory that evolves over measured physical time, demonstrating benefits on timing-sensitive real-robot tasks and LIBERO.

### 1006\. [When Do Discrete Actions Make Good Continuous Controllers?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20429)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20429 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20429)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20429.pdf)]

**Authors:** Zeji Yi, Soham Dasgupta, Xinyang Zhang, ..., Max Simchowitz, Guanya Shi

**Abstract:** Though modern AI models like LLMs predict discrete tokens, today's robotic foundation models represent actions as continuous vectors. In this paper, we challenge this common consensus by asking if continuous action parametrizations are superior to their discrete counterparts. We conduct an extensive comparison of single-step regression and multi-step flow variants of both continuous and discrete policies in both from-scratch single-task and multi-task VLA training, in both simulation and on real hardware. In all regimes, we find that discrete action prediction, even the most naive, uniform-bin quantization, can match or even outperform continuous-action policies in task success rate. Moreover, we find that the challenge to discrete action policies is \emph{not} action precision, but rather factorization error introduced by product-distribution predictions across time and action dimensions. We show that, when paired with the correct interventions---iterative computation through discrete flows, or distribution sharpening through classifier-free guidance---discrete flow policies can turn this weakness into a strength, enabling more responsive action changes during a predicted action chunk. Lastly, we show that attempts to explicitly inject geometric information into discrete policies, e.g. through corruption processes that emulate continuous flow matching, do \emph{not} yield consistent improvements. Taken together, our findings question existing assumptions that robotic action prediction requires distinct design decisions compared to other AI domains, and motivates the possibility of discrete action prediction as a compelling alternative to today's continuous action parametrizations. Link for the webpage \href{https://iscoyizj.github.io/discrete-action-website/}{https://iscoyizj.github.io/discrete-action-website/}

**Comment:** Criterion 1: Through simulated and real-robot experiments including multi-task VLA training, the paper shows that discrete action prediction can match or outperform continuous policies and identifies discrete flows and classifier-free guidance as interventions for factorization error.

### 1008\. [KinoGen: Customizable Humanoid Loco-Manipulation References via Kinematically Grounded Video Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20552)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20552 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20552)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20552.pdf)]

**Authors:** Qi Wu, Lulu Zhao, Sanghyun Hahn, ..., Jiuguang Wang, Kuan Fang

**Abstract:** We present KinoGen, a framework that synthesizes customizable humanoid loco-manipulation references through kinematically grounded video generation. KinoGen conditions a pretrained video generator on calibrated renders of the target robot and object in a simulated task instance and recovers the reference by fitting the same models to the generated two-view video, so that the reference lies in the robot configuration space by construction without a human body proxy or retargeting. Body, hand, and object priors initialize the reconstruction, and direct optimization against cross-view evidence refines it. Since an edited keyframe is simply another rendered state, a user can prescribe a goal object pose, regenerate the video around the edited keyframe, and obtain a reference that satisfies the pose exactly. Across eight loco-manipulation tasks, KinoGen yields lower penetration and foot skating than video-based baselines and preserves over 90% of annotated contacts, and its customized references satisfy every prescribed target with substantially less penetration than direct interpolation. Tracking policies trained on the synthesized references are deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid with dexterous hands. Project webpage: https://kinogen-humanoid.github.io/.

**Comment:** Criterion 1: KinoGen uses kinematically grounded video generation to synthesize humanoid manipulation references for policy training, with tracking policies deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid with dexterous hands.

### 2004\. [Generative Representation Learning for Scalable Latent Tokenization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20533)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20533 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20533)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20533.pdf)]

**Authors:** Alan Baade, Kyle Sargent, Vincent Sitzmann, ..., Li Fei-Fei, Gordon Wetzstein

**Abstract:** We introduce an approach for self-supervised representation learning designed to be used as a tokenizer for generative modeling. Our objective supervises a representation loss by factorizing the pointwise mutual information of the data, which we estimate by taking information differences between conditional predictions from a learned generative model. We justify our algorithm theoretically and empirically, demonstrating that our generative approach to representation learning avoids dimensional collapse and feature suppression in cases where commonly used methods like DINO and LeJEPA fail. Moreover, we show our representation learning technique is scalable; expending more compute leads to predictably better latent tokenization. For the first time, we perform a careful analysis of the combined flop efficiency of tokenizer and generative model and demonstrate that our approach is a compute-optimal improvement over direct diffusion for pixel-space generation, obtaining an FID-50K of 2.18 with JiT-L. Applied to ImageNet and the text-to-image dataset GPIC, we show tokenizer encoder scaling laws for diffusability, where extrapolating our approach achieves representations in 7x fewer FLOPs than DINOv2 on ImageNet and 8x fewer FLOPs than WebSSL on GPIC.

**Comment:** Criterion 2: The paper proposes a self-supervised representation objective that factorizes pointwise mutual information using generative conditional predictions, demonstrating avoidance of dimensional collapse and feature suppression in cases where DINO and LeJEPA fail.

**Abstract:** Feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction builds 3D scenes directly from input images for novel view rendering, but learning Gaussian attributes from predicted geometry for high-quality rendering under large viewpoint changes remains challenging. ### 6007\. [Feed-Forward Multi-View Feature Coding for 3D Gaussian Splatting](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20590)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20590 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20590)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20590.pdf)]

**Authors:** Seungmi Choi, Ji-Hyoun Choi, Suhyun Jo, Tae-Gwon Park, Hui Yong Kim

**Abstract:** Feed-forward 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) methods enable efficient reconstruction of 3D scenes from sparse multi-view images in a single forward pass, without scene-specific optimization. However, existing feed-forward scene-coding pipelines first generate a complete Gaussian representation and then encode its geometry and appearance attributes separately, often involving non-differentiable operations. The resulting 'generate-then-compress' pipeline requires complex attribute-specific codec designs and limits rate-distortion performance by restricting the exploitation of cross-attribute redundancy. We propose multi-view feature coding for Gaussian splatting (MFGS), an end-to-end feed-forward framework for scene coding. Instead of compressing an explicit Gaussian representation, MFGS extracts multi-view features from the input images and encodes them into a compact bitstream. Through end-to-end rate-distortion training, MFGS learns compact multi-view features that jointly represent geometry and appearance, enabling the receiver to predict Gaussian attributes from the decoded features. We further introduce importance-guided Gaussian selection to accommodate different rendering budgets, allowing the receiver to adjust the number of Gaussians used for rendering from the same bitstream. Across RE10K, ACID, and DL3DV, MFGS achieves 99.76-99.86% bitrate savings over the generate-then-compress pipeline, as measured by BD-rate, while reducing total GMACs by 52-55% and providing an approximately 12 times end-to-end speedup.

**Comment:** Criterion 6: MFGS introduces end-to-end feature coding for feed-forward 3D Gaussian reconstruction from sparse multi-view images, jointly encoding geometry and appearance and achieving approximately 12× end-to-end speedup across RE10K, ACID, and DL3DV.

### 6009\. [GLoD-LVSM: Learning Geometry-Grounded Continuous-LoD Scene Representations for Large View Synthesis Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20254)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20254 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20254)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20254.pdf)]

**Authors:** Weikai Lin, Nicholas Milef, Carl S Marshall, ..., Todd Keeler, Yuhao Zhu

**Abstract:** Large view synthesis models (LVSMs) reconstruct scenes from sparse views and render novel views quickly, but they incur unnecessary storage and rendering costs due to token redundancy. We present GLoD-LVSM, a compact, geometry-grounded scene representation for LVSMs with continuous levels of detail. First, instead of learning a fixed set of scene anchor initializations, GLoD-LVSM learns a continuous field that initializes a flexible number of anchors to match the desired budget. A condenser then aggregates scene information into these anchors, with both the field and condenser trained end-to-end. Second, the condenser infers reference-token and anchor geometry to guide compression through geometry-grounded attention, improving rendering quality and enabling frustum culling during decoding. Compared with existing LVSM scene-compression baselines, GLoD-LVSM achieves the best trade-off between rendering quality and scene token count. Compared with the uncompressed base model, GLoD-LVSM reduces the scene-token count by 33.4% and accelerates rendering by $1.23\times$ with no quality loss. Under a relaxed quality-loss budget, the reduction increases to 85.5%, with $3.00\times$ rendering speedup. Further analysis reveals that view-synthesis supervision yields emergent scene depth and self-organized anchors that align with surfaces and adapt their density to local texture complexity. Beyond rendering, we further show that our scene representation also supports scene-semantic understanding, highlighting its potential for broader downstream tasks.

**Comment:** Criterion 6: GLoD-LVSM learns geometry-grounded, continuous-level-of-detail scene representations for sparse-view reconstruction, reducing scene tokens by 33.4% and accelerating rendering by 1.23× without quality loss.

### 7005\. [AutoRSR: Automated Real-to-Sim-to-Real Data Generation for Scalable Robot Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20336)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20336 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20336)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20336.pdf)]

**Authors:** Jiawei Zhang, Chang Liu, Litao Deng, Tao Zhang, Qin Jin

**Abstract:** Scaling embodied robot learning requires large and diverse manipulation datasets, but collecting real-world demonstrations remains expensive in human effort and robot time. Simulation provides a scalable alternative, yet converting a real workspace into a robot-learning-ready environment still requires substantial manual effort for object extraction, asset processing, coordinate calibration, and trajectory specification. We present AutoRSR, a highly automated real-to-sim-to real framework that converts scanned real-world workspaces into reusable embodied simulation assets and scalable manipulation data. Given multi-view observations, robot states, and open-vocabulary semantic queries, AutoRSR reconstructs the workspace, extracts individual object assets, and automatically registers the reconstructed scene to the robot base frame at metric scale. Starting from a small number of reference motions, AutoRSR then generates executable manipulation trajectories and scales them through trajectory interpolation and domain randomization. After workspace scanning and semantic queries, the remaining pipeline requires no manual object annotation, scene-to-robot calibration, or robot teleoperation. Across three real-world manipulation tasks, policies trained on 1K automatically generated episodes per task achieve 73.3--86.7% real-world success rates, comparable to pi 0.5 trained from 150 human teleoperated demonstrations, while reducing the human time required for data generation by an order of magnitude. These results demonstrate that real-world observations can be converted into metrically aligned, executable, and scalable simulation data with substantially reduced human effort.

**Comment:** Criterion 7: AutoRSR automatically converts real workspaces into robot-learning-ready simulation assets and scales manipulation data through trajectory interpolation and domain randomization, achieving 73.3–86.7% real-world policy success with 1K generated episodes per task.

### 7010\. [Hyperspherical Contrastive Representations Unlock Planning and Exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20409)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20409 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20409)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20409.pdf)]

**Authors:** Jonghae Park, Sungil Son, Jonghun Shin, ..., H. Jin Kim, Benjamin Eysenbach

**Abstract:** We develop a self-supervised reinforcement learning algorithm that learns to build towers of up to eight cubes from scratch, without prior data, rewards, demonstrations, or pretrained models. Our method, Contrastive Planning via Spherical Linear Interpolation (C-Slerp), uses contrastive representations as reusable building blocks and repeatedly composes them to infer a sequence of intermediate waypoints. Under our hyperspherical parameterization, the learned representations allow intermediate waypoints to be inferred in closed form through spherical linear interpolation, without training a separate high-level policy or performing graph search. Importantly, the representation geometry also tells us how to adjust the number of waypoints based on the difficulty of each goal-reaching problem. Experiments show that our method succeeds on long-horizon navigation and robotic manipulation tasks, including settings where strong baselines fail to achieve a single success. Ablations further show that using a fixed number of waypoints substantially degrades performance, highlighting the importance of adapting the plan to task difficulty.

**Comment:** Criterion 7: C-Slerp combines self-supervised robot learning with adaptive waypoint planning through spherical linear interpolation, learning to stack towers of up to eight cubes without rewards, demonstrations, or pretrained models.

### 7011\. [PingpongDuo: Agile Humanoid Table Tennis Doubles with Safe Team Coordination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20579)
**ArXiv:** 2610.20579 [[page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20579)] [[pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20579.pdf)]

**Authors:** Haoran Jiang, Yichao Zhong, Kai Zhang, ..., Tianyu Li, Li Chen

**Abstract:** Unlike singles, humanoid table tennis doubles requires teammates to strike in strict alternation while sharing a confined workspace. Rapid repositioning can compromise striking accuracy, while uncoordinated teammate movements introduce collision risks. We present PingpongDuo, a hierarchical framework for agile humanoid doubles with safe team coordination. At the team level, a planner jointly selects positioning targets and uses learned risk estimates to filter risky proposals, preferentially preserving the current striker's target. At the individual level, separate footwork and striking skills combine agile movement with precise racket control.
